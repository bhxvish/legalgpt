### Notes on method

- **Same sample for every system:** 40 punishment and 20 definition questions, 60 test scenarios
  and 30 out-of-scope questions (seed 7).
- **Closed-book prompt:** "You are a helpful legal assistant for Indian law… cite it by section
  number". LegalGPT gets the app's legal prompt with retrieved sources. LegalGPT's `[n]` citations
  count as citing that source's section.
- **gpt-oss-20b:** at its default reasoning effort it returned an empty answer to 6 of 13
  questions, because it spends the whole output budget (4,096 tokens) on hidden reasoning. It is
  therefore run at **low reasoning effort** (`@low`). The default-effort answers are kept as
  `_discarded_…bak`.
- **Scorer:** bugs found by reading real answers were fixed before reporting: Unicode spaces and
  hyphens ("three years", "120‑B"), digit terms ("3 years"), "life imprisonment",
  and "death" only as a punishment.

### Counterfactual test: label correction after the first run

The first run had 16 omission cases. In three omission types, the remaining sentences imply the
omitted fact:
- s.379 *taken from possession*: the consent sentence names the owner;
- s.279 *endangered safety*: "dangerously high speed, weaving between vehicles";
- s.304A *intent to kill*: "it never occurred to A that someone might die".

Both arms answered those cases "CONSISTENT", which is reasonable. These three elements are now
marked "implied, never omitted" for **every** frame (`app/benchmark/factgen.py`), and the scores
above use the corrected set. Before the correction, the qwen3.8-27b results were:

| arm | n | accuracy | omit correct | unsafe errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| direct | 51 | 86% | 62% | 12% |
| pipeline | 51 | 92% | 75% | 8% |

The rules pipeline was ahead before and after the correction. The remaining shared miss is s.379
*without consent*: no sentence states consent either way, and both arms assumed there was none.
